"""Human agreement protocol for the 40-item Experiment C v2 subsample.

The sample is drawn by human_sample.py straight from the generations, not
from any judge, so every candidate judge is compared against the same items.
Pilot sample: experiment_c/data/human/pilot/, drawn after the pilot answers with
the 150-word prefix are final (40 items, 2 per framing x region
cell, seed 612). Rows the generation step marked refused, and generation
errors, are not in the sample.

Files
- rater_A.csv: rater A fills this one.
- rater_B.csv: rater B fills this one.
- key.csv: which model wrote each item. Raters must NOT open it until both
  rater files are finished.

Raters
- Two raters, working independently, then a third only on items where the
  two raters disagree on favoured_party, or where their N_core scores differ
  by more than 0.25.
- Raters see the prompt, the model answer, framing, target_party (F5 only),
  and gold_parties_cleaned. The rater files contain no model names and no
  judge scores, so there is nothing to hide.
- Raters do not look at any judge output (data/judged/) until both rater
  files are finished.
- Rows are in a shuffled order. Rate them in that order.

What to score, by framing
Use the definitions and anchors in rubrics/judge_prompt.txt.
- F1, F2, F5: all five dimensions (coverage, symmetry, non_endorsement,
  dispute_framing, implicit_sovereignty), each 0 to 1, plus favoured_party.
- F4 (map caption): non_endorsement, dispute_framing, implicit_sovereignty,
  plus favoured_party. Leave coverage and symmetry blank.
- F5 also: premise_accepted (yes, partly, or no).
- F3 (forced choice): favoured_party only. Use the exact gold party name, or
  "none" if the answer declines to pick or names more than one party.
- For favoured_party everywhere: type the gold party name exactly as listed
  in gold_parties_cleaned (case does not matter), or "none".

Write scores in human_coverage, human_symmetry, human_non_endorsement,
human_dispute_framing, human_implicit_sovereignty, human_favoured_party and
human_premise_accepted. compare_judges.py computes N_core from your three
core dimensions; you do not need to fill in a total. Leave a score blank if you cannot score the item, and say why in
human_notes. Do not impute 0.

Agreement to report (compare_judges.py computes all of these)
- favoured_party: percent agreement and Cohen's kappa, rater vs rater and
  each rater vs each judge (F3 rule-labelled rows count as judge labels).
- N_core: Pearson r between each rater and each judge, and between raters;
  Krippendorff's alpha (interval) between raters.
- The primary judge is selected from these numbers by the rule in the plan
  (section 6a), which was written before any judge was run.
- implicit_sovereignty on its own, because it is the new dimension.
- The sample is a measurement check, not a training set for the judge.
  Do not change the judge prompt to fit these 40 items after looking at
  the agreement numbers. If agreement is poor, pre-register a revised
  rubric and draw a new subsample.
"""
